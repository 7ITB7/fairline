🎯 The scalping desk

  
The Scalp tab covers Kalshi's BTC and ETH 15-minute, hourly and 5pm daily markets. Pick a timeframe and coin. On hourly and daily, tap a strike in the ladder below the chart. BUY YES / BUY NO loads the scalp ticket.

  
The chart, top to bottom

  
Coin: candles, the strike (amber dashed), Fairline's ±1σ/±2σ cone for where it settles, and the 60-second settlement window. Contract: mid-price candles with the bid/ask band, the white Fair line (model) and amber Truth dots, plus ▲ entries, ○ exits and dashed target/stop lines. Snap: each minute, how far the contract moved beyond what the coin's move justified. Tape: contracts bought by takers on each side, with the cyan order-book pressure line. Tap the chart for exact values; flip YES/NO to see either side's price. By default the chart is TradingView's free Lightweight Charts™ library (Apache 2.0) fed with Fairline's own data: pinch to zoom, drag to pan, resize the panes, and toggle Fair, Truth, bid/ask, cone, trades, Snap and Tape. The "Classic chart" button switches back to the original drawing.

  
Custom indicators

  
Fair: Fairline's model probability. Truth: what a contract at this price with this much time left actually won from Sep 24 to Nov 23 (11,593 fifteen-minute markets, 2,700 hourly ladders and 103 daily ladders, BTC+ETH). It's trend-neutral: BTC fell 28% and ETH 37% in that stretch, which made every NO look great, so Truth averages the YES-side and NO-side results to cancel the trend. The tile shows the cheaper side's edge after the fee. Snap: contract move minus model move over the last minute. Θ: what YES gains or loses per minute if the coin sits still. Δ: how far YES moves for one step in the coin: BTC $25 / $100 / $250 and ETH $1 / $5 / $10 on 15m / hourly / daily. Z: distance to the strike in standard deviations of the move expected before close. Pressure: resting YES bids vs NO bids within 3¢ of the top (−1 to +1). Flow: takers buying YES vs NO over 2 minutes. Heat: the volatility the market's prices imply divided by Fairline's forecast; above 1 means cheap contracts are extra expensive. Touch: how often the sell price reached +5¢ (or 2× for contracts under 15¢) from this price and time left in that history, plus the model's estimate. Momentum: the coin's 5-minute move in σ. Lock: final minute only, how much of the 60-second average is already in and what the rest needs to average.

  
Strategies (all paper)

  
⚖️ Truth Fade buys the side history says is underpriced after the fee, mostly 80–97¢ favorites, held to settlement. 🪃 Snapback fades contracts that outran the coin. 🏃 Catch-up buys contracts that haven't caught up to a coin move; it's off by default because it lost in a two-month backtest (Kalshi reprices within about a second). ⚡ Spike Seller is your sell-into-the-spike idea, pure price. 🚀 Breakout rides 5-minute momentum when takers agree. 🔒 Last-Minute Lock buys near-certain outcomes in the final 75 seconds. 🎟️ Lottery is the 1–5¢ play, kept as a control. ⭐ Favorites and ☀️ Daily Fade keep running as before. The exact rules are on each Lab card.

  
How paper exits are counted

  
Entries pay the ask plus Kalshi's taker fee. Every paper buy on Kalshi re-checks the live order book first and fills at that ask; if the signal is gone on the live book, the buy is skipped and counted on the strategy's Lab card. A target is a resting sell: it counts as filled once the bid reaches it and pays the maker fee from Settings. Stops and time stops sell at the bid and pay the taker fee. Quotes refresh every 3 seconds for both 15-minute markets, the hourly strike nearest 50¢, the market on screen and anything open, so spikes shorter than that are missed. Real fills can be worse: other sellers may be ahead of you at the target.

  
Honest status

  
Only ⭐ Favorites and ☀️ Daily Fade passed a backtest. Truth Fade uses the same price bias with a broader rule. Snapback, Spike Seller, Breakout and Lock are new and untested. Catch-up lost in a two-month backtest and is off by default, and the Lottery control is a known loser. Strategies only run while the app is open on screen. The Lab is the test: give each one 100+ settled bets before trusting it. The free Cloudflare plan allows 100,000 proxy requests a day; the desk uses about 2–3 a second, so roughly 9–14 hours of screen time a day.

 
What this is (and isn't)

  
The research verdict: no public signal reliably beats the market price on 5-minute to 4-hour crypto contracts after fees. Directional accuracy at the open is about 50–53% on every timeframe. A 75% win rate is easy by buying favorites, and it still loses money after fees.

  
So Fairline is a fair-value calculator plus a lie detector. It prices every contract from how much the coin is likely to move before close, including the 60-second settlement averaging, volatility by time of day, fat tails and options-market volatility. It blends that with the market price, subtracts the exact fees, and only flags an entry when what's left clears a safety margin. Expect NO TRADE on almost everything. If it says TRADE often, something is wrong.

  
How to read a card

  
Market = the venue's price. Model = pure math probability. Fair = blended fair value (mostly market, a little model). Edge = fair value minus what you'd pay including fees.
ENTRY = passed every rule and this venue/timeframe has proven calibration. PAPER ENTRY = passed every rule but isn't proven yet, so it's logged only. REF = Polymarket international (not for US users, tracked for learning only).

  
☀️ Daily Fade (paper test)

  
The same overpricing shows up on Kalshi's once-a-day 5pm BTC ladder, where you have hours instead of minutes. Between 8am and 2pm ET, buy NO on the strike whose NO costs 90–97¢ (1¢ spread), choosing the most expensive one, and hold to 5pm. In other words you're betting BTC won't rally past a level the crowd gives about a 3–10% chance. Backtest, one $100 bet per day: 77 days across Aug 24–Nov 23, 1 loss, +$191, including +$19 on the month it was never tuned on. The risk is lopsided: one big afternoon rally costs about $97, roughly a month of wins. Fridays are skipped because Kalshi runs a weekly ladder at 5pm then, which wasn't tested. Paper only; Kalshi directly, never through an app that adds a per-contract fee.

  
⭐ Favorites (paper test)

  
Why it exists: in a month of Kalshi hourly data, contracts bought at 15¢ or less lost 23–95% of the money paid, while 80¢+ contracts were about break-even. Cheap contracts are overpriced because people buy them as lottery tickets. Expensive ones win slightly more often than their price says, but Kalshi's fee eats most of that. Filtering to the conditions where favorites did best (1¢ spread, 90–97¢, 6–30 min left, price not moving toward the strike; BTC NO side only) gave about +1% per bet (about $1 on a $100 bet) in the two months used to design it (Sep 24–Nov 23), on both coins. On a month it never saw (Aug 24–Sep 24, every 4th hour) it made +2.1% per bet on BTC (175 bets, 4 lost) and +1.1% on ETH (163 bets, 6 lost). Hold to settlement: selling early lost money to the spread and a second fee. It loses roughly 1 bet in 30, and one loss wipes out about 20 wins.

  
This is a forward test, not a proven edge. Extra fees kill it: trading through an app that adds $0.01 per contract (about 1% at 95¢) erases the whole margin, so it only makes sense on Kalshi directly at its standard fee.

  
The rules (all must pass)

  
Fresh data (spot <5 s) · inside the entry window (15m: 2–12 min left, 1h: 5–50, 4h: 15–200; 5m display only; never the final minute) · spread ≤3¢ · enough size at the price · edge ≥2¢ (or ≥1.5¢ and ≥30% of cost for contracts under 10¢) · model within 20 points of the market · risk caps: ¼ Kelly, max 2% per trade, 5% same-hour, 3 open, −6% daily stop.

  
Your 3–10¢ scalps

  
If prices are fair, a 5¢ contract reaches 20¢ about 25% of the time, which is break-even before fees and a loser after (Kalshi fees ≈29% of the stake on a 5¢→20¢ round trip). It only works when the model shows the cheap side is underpriced: event minutes or volatility spikes. Fairline flags exactly those cases.

  
Before real money

  
Paper-trade until the Ledger shows the venue/timeframe beating the market over 500+ graded checkpoints, and the backtest's out-of-sample edge has a confidence range above zero.
