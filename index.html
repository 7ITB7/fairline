Calibration: does the model beat the market?

Every market gets logged at fixed checkpoints (NO TRADE calls too) and graded when it settles. "Δ Brier" below 0 means the fair value beat the market price. A venue/timeframe only earns real ENTRY calls after 500+ graded checkpoints with the whole 90% confidence range below zero.

Backtest on settled markets (walk-forward)

Replays real settled markets. It rebuilds the fair value at each checkpoint using only data from before that moment, trains the market-weight on earlier days, and grades it on the next day. Only out-of-sample days count.

What this is (and isn't)

  
The research verdict: no public signal reliably beats the market price on 5-minute to 4-hour crypto contracts after fees. Directional accuracy at the open is about 50–53% on every timeframe. A 75% win rate is easy by buying favorites, and it still loses money after fees.

  
So Fairline is a fair-value calculator plus a lie detector. It prices every contract from how much the coin is likely to move before close, including the 60-second settlement averaging, volatility by time of day, fat tails and options-market volatility. It blends that with the market price, subtracts the exact fees, and only flags an entry when what's left clears a safety margin. Expect NO TRADE on almost everything. If it says TRADE often, something is wrong.

  
How to read a card

  
Market = the venue's price. Model = pure math probability. Fair = blended fair value (mostly market, a little model). Edge = fair value minus what you'd pay including fees.
ENTRY = passed every rule and this venue/timeframe has proven calibration. PAPER ENTRY = passed every rule but isn't proven yet, so it's logged only. REF = Polymarket international (not for US users, tracked for learning only).

  
⭐ Favorites (paper test)

  
Why it exists: in a month of Kalshi hourly data, contracts bought at 15¢ or less lost 23–95% of the money paid, while 80¢+ contracts were about break-even. Cheap contracts are overpriced because people buy them as lottery tickets. Expensive ones win slightly more often than their price says, but Kalshi's fee eats most of that. Filtering to the conditions where favorites did best (1¢ spread, 90–97¢, 6–30 min left, price not moving toward the strike; BTC NO side only) gave about +1% per bet (about $1 on a $100 bet) in the two months used to design it (Sep 24–Nov 23), on both coins. On a month it never saw (Aug 24–Sep 24, every 4th hour) it made +2.1% per bet on BTC (175 bets, 4 lost) and +1.1% on ETH (163 bets, 6 lost). Hold to settlement: selling early lost money to the spread and a second fee. It loses roughly 1 bet in 30, and one loss wipes out about 20 wins.

  
This is a forward test, not a proven edge. Extra fees kill it: trading through an app that adds $0.01 per contract (about 1% at 95¢) erases the whole margin, so it only makes sense on Kalshi directly at its standard fee.

  
The Chart tab

  
Live candles with Fairline's numbers drawn on them: the strike, the coin price now, the green cone (where our volatility model expects the coin to settle), the close and its averaging window, and your paper entries. Below that are the odds over time (market vs model vs fair) and the edge after fees vs the entry bar. Tap 📈 on any signal card to chart that market. The candles and cone are not a buy/sell signal on their own. Only the edge line clearing the bar, with every other rule passing, is.

  
The rules (all must pass)

  
Fresh data (spot <5 s) · inside the entry window (15m: 2–12 min left, 1h: 5–50, 4h: 15–200; 5m display only; never the final minute) · spread ≤3¢ · enough size at the price · edge ≥2¢ (or ≥1.5¢ and ≥30% of cost for contracts under 10¢) · model within 20 points of the market · risk caps: ¼ Kelly, max 2% per trade, 5% same-hour, 3 open, −6% daily stop.

  
Your 3–10¢ scalps

  
If prices are fair, a 5¢ contract reaches 20¢ about 25% of the time, which is break-even before fees and a loser after (Kalshi fees ≈29% of the stake on a 5¢→20¢ round trip). It only works when the model shows the cheap side is underpriced: event minutes or volatility spikes. Fairline flags exactly those cases.

  
Before real money

  
Paper-trade until the Ledger shows the venue/timeframe beating the market over 500+ graded checkpoints, and the backtest's out-of-sample edge has a confidence range above zero.
